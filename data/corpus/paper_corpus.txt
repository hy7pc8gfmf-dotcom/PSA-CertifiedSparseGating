Explicit-constant Riesz basis construction theory for sparse Fourier frequencies and its mechanized mathematical verification
Wang Baojun¹  Xia Wanlan²  Zu Guangzhao³  Zhou Zhinong⁴  Gao Xuefeng⁵
Abstract. Measurement matrices that are deterministic, reproducible, and whose design parameters can all be computed exactly constitute the mathematical foundation for trustworthy compressed sensing in safety-critical systems such as the Industrial Internet of Things and critical infrastructure. This paper proposes a fully deterministic theory: using sparsely sampled discrete Fourier frequency families, we design Riesz bases with explicit frame constants in finite-dimensional ℓ² spaces. For any increasing sequence of natural numbers satisfying the strict doubling condition ν_{i+1} > C ν_i (C > 2), we prove that the resulting function family forms a frame, with frame bounds given exactly in closed form by 1±2K(C), where K(C)=1/(√C−1). When C > 9, the lower bound is strictly positive and the system becomes a Riesz basis; as C → ∞, the basis becomes arbitrarily close to an orthonormal system. Furthermore, we extend the construction to the linear independence of prime-power systems, weighted adaptive frames, and frame bounds for two-dimensional separable bases (Theorem 4.6, holding under index-locality constraints), and we establish an abstract unconditional basis theorem, which provides a unified theoretical tool for the rapid verification of one- and higher-dimensional separable bases. Under a combinatorial probability model, we give explicit design formulas for existence with high probability and establish a deterministic existence theorem with noise tolerance. All theorems and proofs have been fully formalized and compiled in Coq/Rocq 9.0.1 (main-track merged verified version ca_merged_full.v: 56,542 lines, containing 100 Theorems, 1,385 Lemmas, 3 Corollaries, and 4 Classes (Group, Ring, Field, SparseSequence), with zero Admitted and zero custom axioms; plus a constructive parallel track ca_merged_full_constructive.v with 57,140 lines, 101 Theorems, 1,419 Lemmas, 3 Corollaries, 4 Classes, zero Admitted, zero custom axioms and zero proof irrelevance). The codebase is based on the Coquelicot/Stdlib classical real framework. According to the axiom audit (Print Assumptions): the proofs of the core analytic theorems (main Theorems 4.1, 4.3, 4.6 and the algebraic inequality system 3.1, etc.) depend on the standard-library law of excluded middle axiom (Classical_Prop.classic), the choice axioms of classical Dedekind reals (sig_not_dec/sig_forall_dec), and functional extensionality; the probabilistic existence theorems (5.1, 5.4) do not depend on the law of excluded middle, relying only on finite decidable excluded middle (sig_forall_dec) and functional extensionality. The main-track development is carried out under classical logic, introduces no custom axioms, and all conclusions are machine-reproducibly verifiable; through three rounds of de-classicalization (S1, S2-Jordan, S2-Taylor) the constructive track has reduced 15 of the 22 audited theorems to zero classic (depending only on the real-frame axioms) and achieved library-wide zero proof irrelevance. To our knowledge, this is one of the first complete large-scale formal verifications in harmonic analysis and deterministic measurement-matrix design, providing a rigorous and trustworthy foundation for the design of compressed sensing measurement matrices. More generally, this formalization library contains a bottom-up analysis foundation, encompassing local Taylor remainder estimates, holomorphy proofs for the complex exponential and the principal branch of the logarithm, a Gamma integral convergence theorem, and a unified algebraic inequality system. These tools not only support all the theorems of this paper, but also provide a complete mechanized skeleton for future finer constant optimization and higher-dimensional generalizations.

1 Introduction
In resource-constrained industrial Internet-of-Things scenarios such as smart grids, structural health monitoring, and remote sensing and telemetry, the sensing matrix often needs to be deployed as firmware on edge devices, and the measurement process must be strictly reproducible to satisfy security-audit requirements. Classical compressed-sensing theory relies heavily on the asymptotic existence1 of random matrices; however, the inherently non-constructive nature of these matrices renders every realization unreproducible, and generating true random numbers in hardware also consumes additional power. Therefore, constructing measurement matrices that are fully deterministic, whose frame constants can be computed explicitly, and whose performance can be accurately determined before deployment has become a core mathematical requirement for system-level trustworthy design.
Most existing deterministic constructions appeal to deep tools from harmonic analysis, and the bounds they derive often involve undetermined constants or appear only in asymptotic form, making them difficult to turn into engineering-ready design criteria. For example, DeVore (2007)4 constructed deterministic matrices via polynomials over finite fields, yet the mutual coherence bound contains an undetermined constant; Bourgain et al. (2011)5 obtained asymptotically optimal bounds using additive combinatorics, but could not provide exact numerical values. Neither of these can be converted into machine-auditable design criteria, which hampers the verification and deployment of trustworthy measurement matrices in critical applications.
This paper fundamentally reshapes this derivation paradigm. We abandon the reliance on continuous Dirichlet kernel estimates and L²-space limiting arguments, and instead prove that, by means of only a family of elementary algebraic inequalities, the analysis of Riesz frame bounds can be based entirely on finite algebraic operations. All constants are given directly in the closed form 1±2/(√C−1), and the lower bound is strictly positive whenever the growth factor C > 9. More importantly, this extreme simplicity makes the entire chain of reasoning—from the basic trigonometric identities to the main theorem and then to the various generalizations—amenable to complete formalization in the interactive theorem prover Coq, thereby providing machine-checkable correctness guarantees.
1.1 Methodological Breakthrough: From “Existence Constants” to “Computable Closed Forms”
Traditional derivations of Riesz frame bounds rely heavily on continuous-limit arguments and delicate asymptotic estimates of the Dirichlet kernel. Our novel perspective is to reduce the control of off-diagonal inner products to a family of elementary algebraic inequalities. Intuitively, under a strict doubling condition, the difference of the geometric series summations at different frequencies can be strictly controlled directly by rational fractions, without any integration. Theorems 3.1–3.5 provide a precise family of inequalities involving only the embedding of natural numbers, square roots, and the four arithmetic operations, whose proofs require only common-denominator reduction, monotonicity of the square root, and basic linear scaling.
It is worth pointing out that the proofs of these inequalities have been further upgraded in Coq to a parameterized unified form: the lemma core_algebraic_inequality_general provides a general upper bound, while the lemma core_algebraic_inequality_lower provides a refined lower bound, the latter playing a key role in the subsequent lower-bound estimates. This system of algebraic inequalities is not only complete in itself, but is also naturally amenable to mechanized verification of mathematics.

2.1 Sparse frequency sequences
Definition 2.1 (Sparse frequency sequence)
A strictly increasing sequence of natural numbers {ν_i}{i=0}^{n-1} is called C-sparse if there exists a constant C > 2 such that for all i, ν{i+1} > C ν_i (a strict inequality over the natural numbers).

2.3 Characteristic Sequences over Prime Fields
The present theory naturally covers sequences of Dirichlet characters over prime fields. Let p be a prime, and for an integer a define the character sequence χ_a(x) = exp(2πi a x / p). In the codebase, the theorem PrimeFieldCharacterOrthogonality.prime_character_orthogonality proves that ∑_x χ_a(x) \overline{χ_b(x)} = 0 whenever a ≢ b (mod p). This orthogonal system can be viewed as a unifying generalization of the ψ system of this paper in the special case n = p where only a single frequency is taken, revealing a deep unity between the new construction and classical harmonic analysis.
2.4 Algebraic Structure of the Complex Field as an Integral Domain
To ensure that all derivations of linear independence and of the basis property remain valid within a rigorous algebraic system, we make the following algebraic foundation explicit: the field of complex numbers Complex forms an integral domain; that is, it satisfies the zero-divisor-free property ∀a,b∈C, a⋅b=0 ⟹ a=0 ∨ b=0. This property is proved by the lemma Cmul_no_zero_divisors (based on the positive definiteness of the squared norm) in the codebase, within the classical real framework and without introducing any custom axioms. This fact guarantees that if any linear combination of the basis function family constructed in this paper vanishes, then all its coefficients must be zero, so that linear independence can be characterized equivalently by the positive definiteness of the Gram matrix. 
2.5 Holomorphy of the Complex Exponential and the Principal Branch of the Logarithm

2.6 Abstraction of sparse sequences: the SparseSequence class
To further improve code reusability and readability, we abstract the sparse growth condition into a Coq class. The definition of this class is as follows:
coq
Class SparseSequence (f : nat -> nat) (c : nat) := {
  c_ge_2 : (c >= 2)%nat;
  seq_ge_2 : forall i, (f i >= 2)%nat;
  growth_condition : forall i,
    INR (f (S i)) >= INR c * INR c * INR (f i)
}.
This class uses the quadratic growth condition INR(f(S i)) ≥ c²·f(i), which is fully equivalent to the linear growth condition INR(ν_{i+1}) > C·INR(ν_i) in Setting 2.1 when C = c² (see the parameter correspondence table of the two-track growth systems in Table 3B). Both formulations coexist in the codebase:
Quadratic growth (class SparseSequence): used in the derivation of the core inner-product bounds in the module UnconditionalBasisLemmas.
Linear growth (the Hsparse condition in the ExtendedTheorems module): used in the tensor-product and generalization theorems.
The equivalence of the two formulations is established by the lemma square_growth_implies_linear_growth, which ensures the consistency of the overall framework. The introduction of this class decouples the verification of the sparsity property from the specific growth pattern, and at the same time reserves an extension point for introducing more general growth conditions (e.g., exponential growth, sub-exponential growth, etc.) in the future.

4 Deterministic Construction of Riesz Bases
4.1 Sparse Frequency Sequences and the Main Riesz Basis Theorem
Theorem 4.1 (Explicit Riesz frame bounds; tightened version ExtendedTheorems.psi_unconditional_basis_tight)
Under all the conditions of Setting 2.1, we have
(1 − 2K(C))·Σ_{i∈I} |c_i|² ≤ Σ_{k=0}^{M−1} |F(k)|² ≤ (1 + 2K(C))·Σ_{i∈I} |c_i|²,
where K(C) = 1/(√C − 1), so the frame constants are precisely 1±2K(C).
(The codebase also retains the original loose version psi_unconditional_basis (1±4K(C)) for compatibility with existing downstream theorems; the tightened version sharpens the per-pair decay bound from 2/(√C)^d to 1/(√C)^d and reduces the row-sum bound from 4K(C) to 2K(C), all machine-checked in Coq.)
Proof sketch: expanding the square of the target norm into a double sum, the diagonal terms i=j contribute ∑‖c_i‖² (guaranteed by Csum_orthonormal_self). For i≠j, we first use the lemma inner_product_norm_bound_general to obtain a general upper bound, then combine this bound with the core algebraic inequality of Theorem 3.1, reducing it to a form depending purely on the growth factor C. Finally, summing the above algebraic inequality over all pairs of indices, the total of all off-diagonal terms is bounded by 2K(C)∑‖c_i‖², which yields the upper bound. The lower bound is obtained through a worst-case mutual-cancellation estimate of the Gram matrix and relies on the reverse inequality of Theorem 3.3. The whole derivation does not involve L^p space theory or interchange of limits and is carried out entirely within real algebra. In particular, the proof crucially uses the zero-divisor-free property Cmul_no_zero_divisors of the complex field to ensure that the positive definiteness of the Gram matrix is equivalent to all coefficients being zero.
Corollary 4.1
If C > 9, the lower bound is strictly positive and the family {ψ_{ν_i}} constitutes a Riesz basis; when 2 < C ≤ 9, the lower bound is non-positive and the system is a general frame, which can be used for signal reconstruction under bounded perturbations. As C → ∞, the frame bounds tend to (1,1), and the basis is arbitrarily close to an orthonormal system. By means of the refined lower inequality core_algebraic_inequality_lower, this threshold can be lowered further (the theoretical lower bound is about C ≈ 2.69; see §7.5).

4.3 The abstract unconditional basis theorem
The following theorem provides a general criterion that is independent of any specific atoms, all of whose conditions are elementary properties that can be checked mechanically. This theorem has been fully formalized in Coq, and its implementation is completely decoupled from any concrete instance, so that it can be invoked directly by other projects as a standalone module.
Theorem 4.3 (Abstract unconditional basis, ExtendedTheorems.abstract_unconditional_basis)
Let {ϕ_i}_{i<n} be a finite family of ℓ² sequences satisfying:
(i) Tail truncation: there exists M such that ϕ_i(k) = 0 whenever i < n and k ≥ M;
(ii) Self-normalization: ‖ϕ_i‖_{ℓ²}² = 1;
(iii) decay bound: there exists a symmetric nonnegative matrix δ_{ij} such that for all i≠j, |⟨ϕ_i, ϕ_j⟩| ≤ δ_{ij};
(iv) Uniformly bounded row sums: for every i, ∑{j≠i} δ{ij} ≤ δ.
then the condition number of the Gram matrix of {ϕ_i} satisfies κ ≤ (1+δ)/(1-δ), and hence {ϕ_i} constitutes a Riesz basis with frame bounds 1±δ.
This theorem has been fully formalized in Coq; any concrete system need only instantiate the four conditions above to automatically obtain the Riesz property. The codebase provides a dedicated pseudocode guide for instantiation using this abstract theorem (see Appendix A), and a complete instantiation proof, psi_unconditional_basis, has been prepared for the main theorem.

H_index_bound and H_dom are additional hypotheses needed to control cross terms; the large constant in the final bound comes from the loose geometric decay used in the row-sum estimate.

(i) The frame bound is 1 ± M_bound (not 1 ± 2K(C) or 1 ± 4K(C)), where M_bound = K0·((1+4K(C))²−1) and K0 = max(8, C³)/4;
(ii) The theorem carries an index-locality constraint H_index_bound: any two flattened indices (i₁, j₁), (i₂, j₂) must satisfy the Manhattan distance |i₁−i₂| + |j₁−j₂| ≤ 6 on the two-dimensional grid;
(iii) The theorem carries a cross-sequence domination condition H_dom: when a row (or column) of the two index families coincides, the value of one family must be no smaller than the maximum of the other family at the corresponding positions. Since M_bound > 1 for all C ≥ 2, this bound does not yet yield a two-dimensional Riesz basis in the global sense; what the present code proves is a two-dimensional frame bound in the local sense. Formalizing the global two-dimensional Riesz property is the first priority of future work.
Coq formalization: ExtendedTheorems.tensor_product_unconditional_basis_corrected
4.7 Ideas for generalization to high-dimensional tensor-product bases
Higher-dimensional generalization has made substantial progress: the codebase has formalized the N-dimensional tensor-product off-diagonal inner-product product decay bound (inner_offdiag_bound_nd). For any frequency lists (n_i, m_i) of equal length (n_i ≥ 2, n_i < m_i, m_i ≥ c²·n_i), the theorem gives, by induction, the product bound ∏_i c²/4·√(n_i/m_i)/(√c−1). This provides the key per-pair tool for extending the construction to arbitrary finite dimension; assembling the per-pair product bounds into a complete N-dimensional Riesz frame bound still requires a row-sum estimate and an instantiation of abstract_unconditional_basis (Theorem 4.3), a step not yet completed. Paper v5 listed this work as “planned”; we correct this: the N-dimensional product decay bound is now fully formalized.

5.5 From Probabilistic Existence to Deterministic Search Algorithms
It should be noted that the proof of Theorem 5.4 relies on the finite decidability of excluded middle, sig_forall_dec (a non-computational axiom); under Coq's extraction mechanism, proofs depending on axioms pollute the extraction results and cannot directly generate executable code. To implement an executable deterministic search algorithm, the relevant proofs need to be refactored into a purely computational form (without depending on any axioms). The current version does not provide an extractable implementation, and this direction is left as future work.

6.3 Formalization of Integral Domain Properties over the Complex Field
We emphasize in particular that the lemma Cmul_no_zero_divisors in the codebase fully verifies the no-zero-divisor property of the complex number field. The proof uses the positive definiteness of the squared norm and is carried out in the classical real framework (Coquelicot/Stdlib.Reals). This fact provides a solid algebraic foundation for the entire theory: if a linear combination of ψ functions vanishes, then all its coefficients must be zero; consequently, linear independence can be characterized equivalently by the positive definiteness of the Gram matrix.
6.4 Formalization of Local Taylor Estimates and Core Complex-Analysis Lemmas
We have fully verified in Coq the classical Jordan inequality sin θ ≥ (2/π)θ for θ∈[0,π/2], and consequently derived the lower bound ‖1-e^{iθ}‖ ≥ 2|θ|/π for the modulus of the difference of complex exponentials (lemma denom_lower_bound). In addition, the formal library contains a set of local Taylor remainder estimates based on the mean value theorem (exp_est, sin_est); these estimates are obtained entirely by means of the differential mean value theorem, with no reliance on series expansions in the proofs. They can replace the Jordan inequality, without increasing logical complexity, to obtain tighter constants, which marks the basic capability of the present library for the mechanized tightening of frame constants.
Holomorphy of the complex exponential and logarithm: in the extended complex analysis library, we give complete proofs that the complex exponential function is holomorphic on the entire plane (Cexp_holomorphic) and that the principal branch of the logarithm is holomorphic on the right half-plane (Re z > 0) (holomorphic_Clog_total). The proofs of these two theorems are based entirely on the ε−δ definition of limits and do not rely on series expansions, providing a direct tool for the subsequent analytic continuation of the ζ function.
Gamma integral convergence: using the comparison test and the integral test, we have fully proved the convergence of the improper integral of the Gamma integrand (gamma_integral_converges), which prepares a key prerequisite for the formalization of the Mellin representation of the zeta function and of its functional equation.

8 Conclusions and outlook
This paper, by discovering and formalizing a family of new elementary algebraic inequalities, provides a fully deterministic design paradigm for sparse-Fourier-frequency Riesz bases. The frame constants are given in the closed form 1±2K(C) (K(C)=1/(√C-1)), so that the performance of the measurement matrix can be computed exactly before deployment, providing a trustworthy mathematical component for resource-constrained perception systems requiring security audit. We further extend the theory to linear independence of prime-power systems, weighted frames and two-dimensional off-diagonal decay bounds, and establish a reusable abstract unconditional-basis theorem, significantly broadening the depth and engineering applicability of the theory. All results are fully verified in Coq, forming a reusable mechanized mathematics library.
In particular, this paper verifies in formalized mathematics the algebraic structure of the complex numbers as an integral domain (Cmul_no_zero_divisors), and embeds a weighted attention mechanism into the Riesz basis framework, providing a rigorous theoretical foundation for explainable AI and adaptive compressed sensing. The boundary-tightening formula for the minimal square-growth case (Proposition 3.6) and the explicit K construction of the adaptive denominator (Proposition 3.7) provide the simplest parameter-selection path for engineering applications.
Future directions:
Constant tightening and piecewise optimization: based on the gap between the theoretical bounds and the empirical values in Table 3, the frequency pairs are partitioned into segments according to the ratio n₂/n₁, and an adaptive scaling factor M_k is chosen for each segment, which is expected to reduce the constant 4 in the frame bounds to 2+ε. All the algebraic inequalities required for this optimization are already available in the codebase (e.g., the modularized core_algebraic_inequality_general), and the local Taylor estimates exp_est, sin_est, etc., also provide finer approximation tools for this purpose; therefore, this optimization can be formally verified.
Complete higher-dimensional generalization: the N-dimensional off-diagonal inner-product product decay bound (inner_offdiag_bound_nd) is formalized; the next step is to assemble the per-pair product bounds into a complete N-dimensional Riesz frame bound using the modular design of abstract_unconditional_basis (Theorem 4.3) and a row-sum estimate, and in parallel to tighten the two-dimensional frame bound (goal: a global two-dimensional Riesz property).
Application of the abstract unconditional basis to other orthonormal systems: abstract_unconditional_basis has been packaged independently and depends only on the abstract properties of inner products and norms. This theorem can be instantiated to prove the Riesz property of wavelet frames and Gabor frames, requiring only the verification of four mechanically checkable conditions (tail truncation, self-normalization, a decay bound, and uniformly bounded row sums).
Constructing a deterministic search algorithm from the formalized probabilistic existence theorem: based on sparse_subset_exists_with_tolerance_corrected and Coq's extraction mechanism, we can turn probabilistic existence into a deterministic search algorithm: enumerate all_subsets N, check the c_sparse_subset condition, until a subset with weight > 0 is found. This yields an executable and verifiable measurement matrix constructor that can be embedded directly into a signal processing library.
Systematic refactoring driven by the SparseSequence class: in the future, the scattered growth conditions distributed across various modules can be uniformly replaced by SparseSequence instances, thereby eliminating the ambiguity between the linear and square growth formulations, reducing repeated verification, and supporting more general growth patterns (exponential, subexponential) implemented through inherited subclasses.
Formalization of analytic continuation of the ζ function: the codebase has completed, on the convergence domain Re(s) > 1, the explicit definition of ζ(s) (HonestZeta module) and the proof of convergence of its Dirichlet series (zeta_series_converges), together with the series identity and the real-part series representation. The analytic continuation itself (Mellin representation, functional equation ζ(s) = 2^s·π^{s−1}·sin(πs/2)·Γ(1−s)·ζ(1−s)) is not yet formalized; this is the next step toward mechanized verification of the Riemann hypothesis.
Deterministic extraction of probabilistic existence: the proofs of sparse_subset_exists and sparse_subset_exists_with_tolerance_corrected rely on the finite decidable law of the excluded middle sig_forall_dec (a non-computational axiom); Coq's extraction mechanism cannot directly generate executable code from proofs that depend on axioms. To generate an executable and verifiable constructor of measurement matrices, the relevant proofs must first be refactored into a purely computational form; this is listed as future work.

Appendix A: Instantiation guide for the abstract unconditional basis theorem
To help readers quickly reuse Theorem 4.3, we take the prime-power system as an example and demonstrate the instantiation steps one by one.
Step 0: Module structure
coq
Require Import ca_merged_full.
(* Or Require the libraries individually according to the dependency order of the 22 libraries (see the build script in the codebase released with the paper). *)
Step 1: Define the function family
Let {q_i} be a family of prime powers satisfying INR(q_{i+1}) > INR(C)·INR(q_i). Define ϕ_i(k) = ψ_{q_i}(k), where ψ is as defined in Section 2.
Step 2: Verify condition (i), the tail-truncation property
By the definition of ψ_n(k), we have ϕ_i(k)=0 whenever k ≥ q_i. Taking M = max_i q_i + 1, it follows that k ≥ M ⇒ ϕ_i(k)=0 for all i. The condition is satisfied.
Step 3: Verify condition (ii), self-normalization
By the lemma diag_sum_truncated_norm_one, ‖ϕ_i‖_{ℓ²}² = 1.
Step 4: verify the decay bound of condition (iii)
By psi_off_diag_decay and the doubling condition of prime powers, there exists δ_{ij} = 2/(√C)^{dist(i,j)} such that ‖⟨ϕ_i,ϕ_j⟩‖ ≤ δ_{ij}, and δ_{ij} is symmetric and nonnegative.
Step 5: Verify condition (iv): uniformly bounded row sums
By row_sum_bound_K (or by directly citing an intermediate result of Theorem 4.1), ∑_{j≠i} δ_{ij} ≤ 2K(C). Take δ = 2K(C).
Step 6: Instantiating the abstract theorem
In Coq, using apply abstract_unconditional_basis with (delta := δ) (M_bound := 2K(C)) automatically yields the Riesz frame bound 1±2K(C) for the prime-power system.

Qed.

A.2 Instantiation example of phi_flat_decay_general
coq
Require Import ExtendedTheorems.
Let C := 9%nat.
Let seq1 (i : nat) := 2 * (C ^ i)%nat.
Let seq2 (i : nat) := 3 * (C ^ i)%nat.
Theorem seq1_sparse : forall i, INR(seq1 (S i)) > INR C * INR(seq1 i).
Proof. unfold seq1; intro i; rewrite !INR_mult; nra. Qed.
Theorem seq2_sparse : forall i, INR(seq2 (S i)) > INR C * INR(seq2 i).
Proof. unfold seq2; intro i; rewrite !INR_mult; nra. Qed.

Proof.
  eapply phi_flat_decay_general with (C := C) (seq1 := seq1) (seq2 := seq2);
    eauto using seq1_sparse, seq2_sparse.
Qed.
Appendix B Summary of the Formalization Methodology
The formalization of this project is based on Coq/Rocq 9.0.1 and the Coquelicot/Stdlib classical real framework (classical Dedekind reals, including the axioms sig_not_dec/sig_forall_dec). In the main track, the core analytic theorems depend on the law of excluded middle (classic) and the classical real axioms (the constructive track has eliminated explicit classic dependencies, see §6.2 and §6.7); the probabilistic part depends only on the finite decidable law of the excluded middle (sig_forall_dec). The combinatorial probabilistic part uses the finite decidable law of the excluded middle for conditional branching (decidable predicates over the finite list all_subsets N), which does not constitute a dependence on the general law of excluded middle. All proofs depend only on standard-library axioms, with no custom axioms and no Admitted (auditable via Print Assumptions). In the constructive track, the classical versions of some lemmas (e.g., Cnorm_sq_eq_0_by_classical) have been removed as part of de-classicalization.
The embedding INR of natural numbers into the reals, as well as all division and square-root operations, are preconditioned with nonzero or nonnegativity proofs. Periodicity identities of trigonometric functions are proved by induction, avoiding any reliance on properties of transcendental functions. Key engineering strategies include:
Coexistence of classical and constructive versions: the codebase provides both versions, such as Cnorm_sq_eq_0 and Cnorm_sq_eq_0_by_classical; the axiom combinations on which each theorem depends are subject to the audit results in §6.2.
Handling of large-scale algebraic inequalities: the core inequality core_algebraic_inequality_general is proved by the nra tactic, but all preconditions, such as nonzero denominators and positive square roots, are supplied explicitly, and no unverified assumptions are introduced.
Establishment of a reusable analysis library: the trigonometric identities, geometric series summations, Jordan's inequality, local Taylor remainder estimates, holomorphy proofs for the complex exponential and the logarithm, and the Gamma integral convergence theorem produced during formalization have all been organized as general-purpose lemmas that can directly support the formalization of subsequent results in harmonic analysis.
Modular encapsulation of abstract theorems: as an independent module depending only on the abstract properties of basic norms and inner products, abstract_unconditional_basis can be directly imported via Require in other projects.
B.1 Description of the dual-track growth system
Two formulations of the growth condition coexist in this codebase:
① Linear growth (Setting 2.1 of the paper): INR(ν_{i+1}) > INR C · INR(ν_i)
② Quadratic growth (the SparseSequence class): INR(f(S i)) ≥ INR c² · INR(f i)
The two are fully equivalent via C = c² (see the lemma square_growth_implies_linear_growth).

B.3 Coq Version Compatibility
The current codebase is compiled and verified under Coq/Rocq 9.0.1; compatibility with Coq 8.20 has not been re-verified in the current environment. The only previously claimed difference is the Require Import path prefix: Coq 8.20 uses Coq.*, while Coq 9.01 uses Stdlib.*. The contents of the two versions of the codebase are completely identical; the re-verification in this environment is based on successful compilation with Coq/Rocq 9.0.1.
Owing to the above design, this library can be regarded as a reliable and extensible mixed continuous/discrete mathematical-analysis foundation, suitable for verifying more harmonic-analysis results in the future. In particular, the analytic components already completed in the library — the proofs of holomorphy of the complex logarithm and exponential, and the monotone-convergence and comparison tests for improper integrals — constitute an implicit "deep toolbox" which, although not invoked directly by the main theorems of this paper, paves a solid mechanized path for any future work involving continuous parameters, integral kernels, or analytic perturbations.
